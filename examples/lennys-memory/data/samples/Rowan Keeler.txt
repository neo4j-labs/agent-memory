Rowan Keeler (00:00:18):
Thanks for having me. I have been looking forward to this since we met at the conference in Berlin.

Host (00:00:31):
Let's start where everyone wants to start: product-market fit. Northwind pivoted twice before it worked.

Rowan Keeler (00:00:44):
We did. The first version was a dashboard for warehouse managers. It demoed beautifully and nobody renewed. The lesson was that a dashboard is not a job to be done.

(00:01:12):
The second version shipped as an alerting product, and that is when retention finally moved. We went from twelve percent monthly retention to sixty-one percent in two quarters.

Host (00:01:40):
What did you change about how you hired during that period?

Rowan Keeler (00:01:51):
We stopped hiring for pedigree. Our best product manager came from a freight brokerage in Hamburg and had never worked at a software company. She understood the customer better than any of us.

Host (00:02:20):
You have written about the "two-week rule" for roadmaps. Explain that.

Rowan Keeler (00:02:29):
Nothing goes on the roadmap unless someone can ship a slice of it in two weeks. If a project cannot be sliced, we do not understand it yet. That one rule killed more bad ideas than any review process we ever ran.

Host (00:03:05):
Last question. What is the advice you would give a founder in their first year?

Rowan Keeler (00:03:14):
Talk to fifty customers before you write a line of code, and write down what you expected them to say before each call. The gap between your prediction and their answer is where the company is.
