= Ontologies
:description: What an ontology is, how it extends POLE+O, how it drives extraction and resolution, and how validation modes and versioning work.

Ontologies work on *both* backends. As of v0.7 `client.ontology` is backed by
`BoltOntology` on bolt (versioned `:Ontology` / `:OntologyVersion` nodes in your
own Neo4j) and by `NamsOntology` on the hosted service. The two implementations
satisfy one `OntologyAPI` Protocol, so ontology-management code reads the same
either way — see <<bolt-vs-nams>> for where the behaviour differs.

== What is an ontology?

An *ontology* is a typed, versioned, validated schema for your knowledge graph.
Where the base xref:explanation/poleo-model.adoc[POLE+O model] gives you five
coarse entity categories (Person, Organization, Location, Event, Object), an
ontology refines them into a concrete domain vocabulary:

* **Entity types** — each carries a `pole_type` (one of the POLE+O categories),
  an optional `subtype`, display metadata (`color`, `icon`), and a list of typed
  **properties**.
* **Properties** — a `name`, a `type` (`string`, `datetime`, `date`, `float`,
  `integer`), and optional `required`, `unique`, and `enum` constraints.
* **Relationships** — a typed edge (`UPPER_SNAKE`) with `source` and `target`
  entity labels.

NAMS ships ~28 *system templates* (healthcare, legal, cybersecurity,
financial-services, software-engineering, and more). Activating one makes
server-side extraction produce a clean, predictable graph — `Patient`,
`Provider`, `Diagnosis` with enforced fields and enumerations — instead of
generic, loosely-typed nodes. The bolt store ships the eight local domain
templates instead (`poleo`, `podcast`, `news`, `scientific`, `business`,
`entertainment`, `medical`, `legal`), built from the same catalog the extractors
use.

== One document, four consumers

On bolt the ontology is not only a validation schema — it is the single thing
the extraction and storage pipeline agrees on. The effective document is
resolved once per connection and handed to:

. **GLiNER2.5's JointIE schema**, compiled from the document. Entity
  descriptions become zero-shot annotation guidelines, and relationship
  `source`/`target` labels are enforced *during* beam search rather than
  filtered afterwards.
. **The LLM extractor's prompt**, where the same descriptions and the legal
  `SOURCE -[REL]-> TARGET` catalogue replace a hardcoded POLE+O block.
. **Relation validation** — `ExtractionResult.validate_relations()` drops
  relations whose endpoint labels the ontology does not permit, which matters
  most for the stages that have no endpoint typing of their own.
. **Entity resolution** — `EntityTypeDef.aliases` is the resolver's gazetteer,
  and `resolution_threshold` / `review_threshold` are its per-type bands.

Hence the extension fields on the document model: `description` on entity types,
relationships and properties; `aliases`; `threshold` (a per-label decoding
floor); `resolution_threshold` and `review_threshold`; and the decoding
constraints `unique_source`, `unique_target`, `acyclic`, `allow_self`, `inverse`
and the document-level `no_self_loops`. They are all defaulted, so a document
written before v0.7 (or fetched from NAMS) still parses.

Two design choices follow from JointIE's flat label space. Fine-grained types
are declared as *labels* with a `pole_type` and a `subtype` rather than
recovered by a second pass, and `RelationshipDef.source`/`target` stay single
labels — several defs may share one `type`, and the compiler groups them into
one relation with head/tail lists.

== Templates vs. owned ontologies

Templates are read-only (`is_system = true`). To customize one you *clone* it
into an editable copy, which you can then revise. Exactly one version is active
at a time: a workspace on NAMS (where `nams-default` is implicitly bound until
you activate your own), a database on bolt (where nothing is bound until you
activate something, and the library falls back to the built-in POLE+O ontology).

On bolt, templates are synthesised rather than stored: they carry an id of
`template:<name>`, have no revision history, and cannot be updated or deleted.
`clone("podcast")` is what turns one into a real, editable ontology at revision
1.

== Versioning and immutability

Every change produces a new *immutable revision*. `clone` yields revision 1;
each `update` yields revision n+1. A version records its `revision`,
`validation_mode`, a `schema_hash`, and provenance. You `activate` a specific
*version* (by its `ov_…` id) — activation is what binds a schema to the
workspace and starts validating writes against it.

== Validation modes

Each version has a `validation_mode`:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Mode | Behavior
| `permissive` | (default) Non-conforming entity writes are accepted and recorded.
| `strict` | Writes that violate the schema are rejected. The SDK surfaces the rejection as `ValidationError`.
|===

`permissive` favors ingestion tolerance; `strict` favors graph cleanliness. You
choose per version, and `get_active()` reports the active mode.

On bolt the mode is enforced client-side, and one rule holds in *both* modes:
relations the ontology does not permit are dropped before storage, because
writing an edge the schema forbids is never the intent. What `strict` adds is
entity-level enforcement — `long_term.add_entity` and `add_relationship` raise
`ValidationError` for an undeclared type, an undeclared relationship name or a
forbidden endpoint pair, and message ingestion drops extracted entities whose
`(type, subtype)` the ontology does not declare. The mode comes from
`schema_config.validation_mode`, then the active stored version's mode, then
`strict` if `strict_types` else `permissive`.

== Bolt vs NAMS

The method surface is identical; the mechanics behind it are not.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Concern | Bolt (`BoltOntology`) | NAMS (`NamsOntology`)

| Storage
| `(:Ontology)-[:HAS_VERSION]->(:OntologyVersion)` in your database, with the
  document as a JSON string property and a `schema_hash` over its content
| Server-side, per workspace

| Templates
| The eight local domain templates, synthesised as `template:<name>` rows
| ~28 system templates with revision rows

| Active version
| One per database. Nothing is bound until you `activate()`
| One per workspace. `nams-default` is bound implicitly

| `get_active()` with nothing bound
| Raises `NotSupportedError` (it does *not* silently fall back to POLE+O — the
  runtime ontology resolution applies that fallback itself)
| Raises `NotSupportedError`

| `import_` formats
| Converted locally: `json`, `yaml`, `native`, `arrows`, `auto`. The
  extraction-backed converters (`rdf`, `graphql`, `cypher`, `linkml`,
  `data-importer`) and any `url=` fetch raise `NotSupportedError`
| Every format, including server-side URL fetch

| `migrate`
| Runs **synchronously**, one transaction per label pair, and returns an
  already-terminal `MigrationJob`. `batch_size` is accepted and ignored
| Enqueues a background job you poll with `get_migration()`

| Validation
| Client-side, from `validate_structure()` plus the strict write paths
| Server-side, at write time

| Extension fields (descriptions, aliases, thresholds, constraints)
| Drive the local pipeline: the JointIE schema, the LLM prompt and the resolver
| Client-side concepts. `NamsOntology` sends the document exactly as given, but
  whether the service persists fields it does not model is not verified for
  this release — extraction and resolution there run against its own engine

| Drives extraction
| Yes — locally, once resolved at connect time
| Yes — server-side, asynchronously after a write
|===

== Relationship to extraction

On *NAMS*, ontologies shape the server-side GraphRAG extraction pipeline. Once a
version is active, entities extracted from stored messages — and entities you
write directly with `add_entity` — are validated against the active schema.
Extraction is asynchronous; use
xref:how-to/use-ontologies.adoc[`wait_for_extraction`] when you need to observe
the result deterministically.

On *bolt*, extraction runs in your process, so the ontology is resolved once at
connect time and compiled into the extractor before the first message is stored.
Precedence: the explicit `MemoryClient(ontology=...)` keyword,
`schema_config.ontology_path`, `schema_config.custom_schema_path`, the active
stored version (when `use_active_ontology` is on), `SchemaModel.CUSTOM` plus
`entity_types`, then the built-in template named by `ontology_template`. Because
that happens at connect time, activating a version affects the *next*
connection, not the client that activated it. `client.ontology_document` and
`client.validation_mode` report what was resolved.
